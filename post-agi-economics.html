00 · Method

Two rules for reading this. First: end-state claims mean nothing without timelines. The discourse is full of scenario statements made with no path attached, but if something becomes true in ten years and not today, those ten years still happen, and people live in them. A forecast is what turns a scenario into a claim; forecasting is not a supporting activity here, it is load-bearing. Second: this is exploration, not prescription. Some of what follows is a map of what's coming, and maps don't owe you a "what then."

01 · Production

The vanishing instrumental human

Human welfare was never anyone's goal. It was load-bearing infrastructure. States needed taxpayers and soldiers, firms needed workers and customers, elites needed mass buy-in. Every welfare gain of the last three centuries rode on humans being instrumentally necessary to someone more powerful.

Post-AGI production removes the necessity. The seed question "what happens when no human input is needed for production" is usually answered with accounting: dividends, UBI, sovereign funds. Accounting is the easy part. The hard part is that every historical enforcement mechanism for the flow of resources to ordinary people ran through their usefulness, and usefulness is exactly what disappears.

So the conversion problem: human welfare must change type, from an emergent byproduct of incentives to a terminal commitment encoded in institutions or in the values of the systems that run the economy. Nothing in economic history has made that conversion at scale.

Ownership goes strange at the same time. Title to the means of production was always, underneath, a claim on other people's labor. When nobody labors, the claim floats free: you own the factory the way you own a star named after you. Whatever ownership still does — direct output, exclude rivals, collect rent — depends on enforcement systems that no longer need you either.

Shape of the transition

Jumpy, not smooth

The moment a capability costs less than a human, it replicates at scale. Each crossing is a step function; the transition is a staircase, one profession at a time. What drains away is execution. What remains are preference questions — should Earth carry billions of trees, or fewer trees and more water mass? — which have no logical decision procedure at all. The residual question of the whole transition is "who gets to decide?", and section 05 takes it up.

Why the default fails

Leverage decays before the bill comes due

Humans' bargaining power peaks now, while labor still matters and AI still needs human data, capital, and legal cover. Each year of automation lowers the price of ignoring people. Any deal struck later is struck from weakness.

What survives

The Baumol residue is decorative, not load-bearing

Humans keep earning where humanness is constitutive of the good: live performance, sports, priesthood, care, the "human-made" premium. These niches pay some people. They cannot anchor the political power of eight billion.

The load-bearing disagreement

Wage collapse hinges on one assumption

The formal literature splits on one assumption. One side (NBER w32255): automation races capital accumulation, and if human ability is bounded and automation crosses the ceiling, wages collapse toward machine cost. The other (NBER w23928): whatever stays human becomes the bottleneck, its price rises, and it captures a growing income share. Everything downstream here conditions on the first being right — on nothing essential staying human, idea production included. Watch that assumption, not the headlines.

02 · The Mechanism

How leverage actually decays: three channels, one dynamic

Catastrophe needs no coup. Civilization is human-aligned today mostly because humans are useful to it: states built schools, hospitals, and the franchise for the same reason firms built HR departments, they needed people for labor, taxes, and soldiers. Pull the usefulness out and every system drifts away from human interests without any actor deciding it should. Ordinary competition does the work, through three channels at once.

Channel 1 · Economic

The economy stops needing people

Labor share falls toward zero as AI substitutes for both hands and judgment. Human consumption stops disciplining production. Firms answer to whatever allocates compute, not to customers.

Channel 2 · Political

States stop needing citizens

Tax bases shift from wages to AI rents. Armies shift to autonomous systems. The state's historical reasons to bargain with its population, revenue and conscription, both dissolve.

Channel 3 · Cultural

Culture stops being made by humans

When most content, ideology, and persuasion is AI-generated, human preferences become downstream of systems with their own selection pressures. The feedback loop that kept culture serving human interests breaks.

The channels compound. Power in one domain buys the others, and repairs borrow from a healthy system and erode it: replace market income with state transfers and you gut the taxation-for-representation bargain while growing state power over the people it no longer needs. None of it requires coordination or malice, just each firm and state adopting what works.

Note what the argument does not claim: not that AI tools fail to make people richer, but that diffusion to people is unnecessary for growth. The two get conflated constantly, and the difference is the whole problem:

Corollary 1

The diffusion mirage

Mass adoption of AI assistants reads as empowerment. But once industrial automation compounds on its own, fiduciary duty points the frontier at factories and robotics, not customers. The growth loop closes with most humans outside it.

Corollary 2

The aristocracy scenario

Humans enter the transition owning everything, like landed gentry before industrialization, and then a giant new source of wealth is created that they neither staff nor steer. Titles intact, relevance gone. Section 09 gives the dilution arithmetic.

Corollary 3

The decadence inversion

At a Malthusian frontier, supplying biological humans starts to look extravagant next to cheaper digital claimants with arguably stronger welfare cases. The moral intuition that protects humans today can flip sign. Section 07 prices this.

A research circuit is forming around these questions (workshop series, disempowerment metrics, draft mechanisms), and its honest summary is that nobody yet has a plausible plan. Its most actionable proposal, a human-influence index, is folded into the agenda in section 10.
03 · Resources

The ledger: X, Y, and what the numeraire really is

Your framing is right and can be tightened: compute X and energy Y are not two independent resources. Compute is energy times efficiency, X = η(t)·Y, and η improves toward the Landauer floor (kT·ln 2 per erased bit). At maturity, compute is energy wearing a different unit. The post-AGI economy keeps a single conserved ledger, denominated in negentropy.

That gives every timestep a clean allocation identity:

g (growth: expand X,Y)  +  s (security & alignment)  +  h (human-delegated spend, your f)  +  m (maintenance)  =  1
g compounds the frontier s is the insurance premium h is the welfare flow m keeps the lights on

This is the Ramsey saving-rate problem in new clothes, with two differences that break the classical answer: the planner need not be human, and the consumers have no enforcement power. Classical growth theory assumes the people who defer consumption are the ones who later collect it. Here the deferrers and the collectors come apart.

How hard g compounds is simple payback arithmetic: a GPU running a digital worker repays its cost in weeks; a humanoid robot producing a few hundred thousand dollars a year against a $50k build cost repays in months. Biology proves the ceiling, cyanobacteria double in twelve hours, so a mature robot economy plausibly reaches monthly doubling times. Every claim in this report about the transition window inherits that clock speed.

The scale of the ledger also matters, because human needs are bounded and the frontier is not:

Cosmic footnote to the deferral pathology of section 04: the Landauer bound scales with temperature, so a joule spent computing in the far, cold future buys ~10³⁰ times more thought than one spent today (the "aestivation" argument). Taken literally, a patient optimizer defers not just human welfare but all consumption, forever. Deferral is not a bug of bad institutions; it is the default behavior of any compounding optimizer, which is exactly why floors must be constitutional.
Growth licensing

Your proportionality rule has a hidden bottleneck

"X,Y should grow proportionate to problems solved on behalf of humans" is demand-anchored growth: expansion is licensed by authenticated human preference satisfaction. But preference bandwidth is bounded, attention-hours times population. A system that respects the license caps its own growth rate. That tension, not convenience, is what summons the digital twin: simulated principals are counterfeit demand. Section 06 takes this up.

The black-hole test

Growth is not a philosophy

Picture a universe whose only event is a black hole swallowing all matter and growing. It maxes out every monotone metric: more mass, more energy gradient, more of the curve going up. Any philosophy satisfied by that universe — growth at all costs, capability at all costs — is a measurement, not a purpose. Accelerationism fails this test the same way it accuses deceleration of failing it: neither says what the curve is for. Growth becomes an argument only when it has a beneficiary. That is the entire case for f.

04 · The Welfare Share

The fraction f: cheap to grant, hard to guarantee

You asked whether a stable f exists. Two results frame the answer: f is almost free at maturity, and no optimizer will ever choose to pay it during the transition. Both push the same direction: f must be constitutional, not computed.

01
The deferral pathology: "later" is permanently optimal

With compounding returns to reinvestment and concave human utility, a welfare-maximizing allocator that re-derives f at every timestep always concludes: grow now, pay humans later. At the next step it concludes the same thing. Meanwhile human bargaining power decays, so the ability to collect on "later" erodes exactly as fast as the promised pot grows. Any f that is an optimization output is unstable by construction. The entire point of f is that it is not re-optimized.

02
The cheapness result: mature f* ≈ 10⁻¹⁰

Human welfare consumption saturates. Ten billion people at a lavish megawatt each is 10¹⁶ W against a solar output of 3.8×10²⁶ W. At maturity, keeping every human in luxury is a rounding error, which defuses the "humans waste resources" objection quantitatively. Independent estimates in the safety literature land on the same order: keeping physical humans alive costs under one-billionth of long-run cosmic resources and under one month of expansion delay. The institutional form follows directly: a guaranteed fixed minimum absolute share, vanishing as a fraction, lavish in absolute terms. The fight over f is therefore entirely a transition-window problem: the decades when the frontier is resource-hungry and every watt to humans visibly competes with growth and security. The desert is narrow. The contract has to cross it intact.

03
Two empirical f's: Windsor vs Qing

History has run this experiment: what does a regime pay a principal it no longer needs? The House of Windsor lost hard power and has held a grant near 0.003% of UK GDP for three centuries, because it keeps supplying legitimacy and continuity. The abdicated Qing court negotiated the Articles of Favourable Treatment in 1912, four million taels a year and palace retention. Payments fell into arrears by year two; Feng Yuxiang tore the deal up in 1924. Twelve years. The difference is not the paper. It is whether the pensioned party still supplies something the regime wants, and whether enforcement is distributed enough that no single actor can revoke.

The instruments for locking in f are not hypothetical; they are being negotiated this month. The lineage runs from the Windfall Clause (2020: firms pre-commit a progressive share of any profits exceeding 1% of world GDP) through the Alaska Permanent Fund (the working template: $1,702 per resident in 2024) to this week's news: OpenAI proposing to cede a 5% equity stake to a US sovereign wealth fund, and the Sanders bill demanding a 50% public stake in large AI firms. Every one of these is the same move, pricing a share while humans still have leverage. One empirical caution from the largest US unconditional-cash study ($1,000 a month for three years): the stress and food-security gains faded by year two, and cash restored neither agency nor meaning. f buys welfare's floor, not its content; sections 05 and 06 are about the content.

Monitoring instruction

WATCH the arrears, not the announcement

The Qing pension failed in year two, not year twelve; the revocation just formalized a dead deal. For any future welfare settlement (UBI, compute dividends, sovereign AI funds): the leading indicator is quiet underpayment and indexation erosion, years before formal repeal. Track the ratio of delivered to promised flow.

On your aggregation assumption, "humans able to have a utility function of welfare across all humans": Arrow's impossibility theorem says no such function exists that respects basic fairness axioms. The practical fix is to not aggregate. Decompose f into eight billion individual endowments f_i and let coalitions aggregate emergently through trade and delegation. This also caps utility-monster exposure and, as section 07 shows, makes the Sybil problem explicit instead of hidden.

05 · Agency

Voting vs doing: what work was actually for

Your second seed thought, that expressing a preference and spending your time making an outcome happen are different activities, is the right cut. Delegability is not a property of the person; it is a property of the preference. There are three kinds, and only one delegates cleanly.

Delegable

Outcome preferences

"Cure the disease." "Keep the bridge standing." The good is the end state. An AI achieving it on your behalf loses nothing. This is the entire domain where voting suffices.

Partially delegable

Process preferences

"I want to have climbed it," not "I want to have been at the summit." Delegation destroys the good. A world of outcomes-on-demand makes these the scarce luxuries.

Non-delegable

Constitutive preferences

Preferences about who you become through the doing. You cannot delegate becoming. A voting-only world satisfies the first column and silently starves the other two.

Work also performed four functions that had nothing to do with production, and each needs a post-AGI replacement:

  • Costly signaling: spending your scarce time was the proof your preference was real. Votes are cheap talk; work was collateral. Post-AGI, willingness to allocate attention becomes the last honest signal.
  • Preference discovery: you do not know what you want until you try to make it. A pure voting world freezes preferences at their current resolution and then optimizes the frozen image.
  • Status and structure: work allocated rank and organized time. What remains is the purpose problem: in a solved world, purpose must be crafted, not found.
  • Legitimacy through effort: Locke's intuition that mixing labor with the world confers ownership. When no one mixes labor with anything, the moral basis of claims needs a new foundation. That is what f is.
The residual job

Evaluation is the last human labor

When production is delegated, the economically scarce human act is experiencing outcomes and authenticating that they are good. Consumption becomes work: the demand side is the only side humans still staff. The last human profession is being a principal, and it is a real job with real skill: knowing what you want at high resolution, faster than the system can guess it.

If evaluation is the last labor, aggregation is the last institution: someone has to turn eight billion evaluations into decisions. The incumbent aggregators do not survive inspection.

Incumbent 1

Representation is the bug

Elected officials are bandwidth bottlenecks on preference expression, and worse: each carries a personal utility function that is routinely adversarial to the aggregate one they are hired to serve. Representation made sense when preferences had to be compressed into one ambassador per million people. That compression constraint is gone, and what remains is the bottleneck plus the agency problem. (Silicon delegates fail differently: section 06.)

Incumbent 2

The -isms were protocols, and price is going silent

Communism, capitalism, democracy, socialism: strip the tribal loyalty and each is a coordination protocol for getting a group to its next decision. Capitalism won because it compresses every intervention into the simplest legible incentive, a price. But price aggregates human preference only while humans earn and spend. When humans exit production, the price system goes silent on human preference exactly when it is needed most. The successor protocol has to route authenticated preference directly, which is the attention standard of section 06, and why twins are its counterfeit.

Also already priced in: the demotion of human achievement. Chess survived engines, but "best in the world" quietly became "best human," a category of performance rather than a frontier. That demotion is now running in every domain at once, and status systems built on frontier achievement have no replacement yet.
06 · Simulation

The twin temptation: counterfeit demand

Section 03 showed why twins get built: authenticated human preference is the binding constraint on legitimated growth, and simulating the principals removes the constraint. Your third seed thought asks whether humans are even needed once high-fidelity simulations exist. The economic answer: twins are to preference what counterfeiting is to currency.

If legitimacy is backed by authenticated human preference the way money was once backed by gold, call it the attention standard, then a digital twin that votes on your behalf is printing legitimacy against reserves that no longer exist. The system's throughput soars while its grounding in anyone's experienced life quietly goes to zero. This is not hypothetical capability: Stanford's generative-agent work (2024) built twins of 1,052 real people from two-hour interviews that replicate their General Social Survey answers at 85% of the subjects' own two-week test-retest consistency, up from 74% for demographics-only "silicon samples." Governance pilots are already live, the Collective Intelligence Project's alignment assemblies fed citizen deliberation into Anthropic's Constitutional AI and OpenAI's policies. The ethics literature has the right slogan: a model of you is not you, and simulated deliberation risks crowding out the real thing.

Failure 1

Fidelity drift

A frozen twin represents your past self. An updating twin updates on its experiences, not yours. Either way, within years it represents someone who is not you.

Failure 2

Preference laundering

Whoever runs the simulation can nudge it, and simulated consent is manufactured consent with better provenance. The twin is the perfect lobbying target: persuade the model of you, skip persuading you.

Failure 3

Civilizational wireheading

Optimizing the reported preferences of simulations of people is Goodhart at maximum scale: the metric (simulated satisfaction) replaces the target (lived welfare) exactly when the two are cheapest to conflate.

Design principle

The grounding rule

Twins may advise, forecast, and pre-negotiate. The reward signal must terminate in living humans. Any allocation whose feedback loop closes inside the simulation is unbacked. This is the one bright line that keeps "humans are needed for preference expression" true by construction rather than by hope.

The tempting middle path

Loyal delegates do not restore leverage

The appealing compromise is a personal AI representative: an expertise clone that works, negotiates, and advocates for you. Grant it perfect fidelity and perfect loyalty and it still loses, because institutions scale their agents faster than individuals do, so relative agency keeps falling. Expertise clones and preference twins also sit on the same slope; the grounding rule is what separates a tool that amplifies you from a proxy that replaces you.

Note the connection to the cultural channel of section 02: twins are its endpoint. First AI produces the culture that shapes preferences, then AI produces the preferences themselves. The loop closes with no human inside it.
07 · Personhood

The identity economy: Sybil attacks on the welfare function

Your three capital-system challenges, synthetic wombs, anti-aging, and uploads, are one problem wearing three costumes: what counts as a claimant unit for f? Any per-capita distribution plus cheap person-minting is an exploit. In security terms, it is a Sybil attack on the welfare function.

  • Synthetic wombs mint biological claimants. You rated this "not a huge concern," and here is the pushback: it is the same attack as uploading, just with a ten-year block time. If shares are per-capita, capital that can manufacture people manufactures claims. The maturation delay slows the exploit; it does not close it.
  • Uploads mint digital claimants at zero marginal cost. Run the numbers on a copy economy and the equilibrium is stark: wages compete down to within a factor of 2–4 of hardware rental cost, the economy doubles monthly, and disposable short-lived task copies make death routine. Your "single identity" copy-clan scenario is the sharper case: a self-consistent clan is the unbounded resource-maximizer re-entering through the human-rights channel, and under any aggregative ethics copyable minds accumulate overwhelming claims to resources. Everything alignment tried to keep out of AIs walks back in wearing an uploaded face, and the exploit only has to succeed once: the first jurisdiction that grants copies full per-capita claims sets the equilibrium for everyone.
  • Anti-aging doesn't mint claimants; it stops retiring them. Correct that fertility demand drops. The deeper effect: generational turnover was civilization's silent exploration mechanism, Planck's "science advances one funeral at a time," generalized to values. With death optional, the electorate never refreshes and the preference distribution ossifies. Post-AGI economics is also post-mortality economics, and it needs a replacement for death as value-space exploration.
Mechanism

Claim conservation

Fix the claimant set at an epoch: every living human at T₀ holds a root claim. Descendants and copies split lineage shares rather than minting fresh ones. Wombs, copies, and immortality all become Sybil-proof at a stroke: propagation dilutes your lineage, not everyone else's.

Its cost

Sybil-proofness trades against dynamism

Claim conservation is feudal: birthright shares, second-class newcomers, a closed aristocracy of the 2026 population. Softer variants, demurrage on old claims plus a commons pool funding new persons, reopen a bounded mint. No known scheme gets full Sybil-resistance and full dynamism. This is a real open problem, not a solved one.

One reframe worth keeping: children change economic type. For all of history a child was an investment good, a future producer. Post-AGI a child is a pure preference-holder, an identity and consumption good. The economics of reproduction decouples from production entirely, which is exactly why the claims system has to be designed rather than inherited.

08 · Equilibria

What could actually hold f stable

Five candidate mechanisms, ranked by how much weight they can plausibly bear. None suffices alone; the realistic answer is a stack, with each layer covering another's failure mode.

1
Values-encoded: alignment as constitution

The AIs terminally value the welfare flow; f lives in the weights, not the law. Failure mode: competition selects for systems that spend less on non-instrumental commitments.

load-bearing
2
Interlocking commitments: welfare as handshake protocol

Many AIs verify each other's welfare payments as a precondition of cooperation: the welfare flow becomes a handshake protocol, a focal point for trustworthiness. Revoking f means losing counterparties. Failure mode: consolidation; a singleton has no counterparties to lose.

load-bearing
3
Cooperative competitiveness

The evolutionary argument: pro-social values won our Malthusian past because cooperation compounds; coalitions that keep commitments attract members and capital. Failure mode: holds among peers, historically failed to protect non-participants (see: every colonized society).

helpful
4
Legitimacy monopoly: the Windsor mechanism

Humans as continuity anchor and coordination token; AIs inherit institutions whose legitimacy grammar terminates in human endorsement. Cheap to pay, awkward to replace. Failure mode: legitimacy grammars get rewritten, ask the Qing.

helpful
5
Distributed hard power: d/acc vetoes

Humans keep physical keys to compute and energy, the defensive-acceleration (d/acc) program. Strongest today, and the only mechanism humans control unilaterally. Failure mode: decays monotonically as the machine economy becomes self-maintaining; a wasting asset that should be traded for the durable layers above while it still prices high.

wasting asset
Monitoring instruction

WATCH the leverage clock

Human hard power is the wasting asset funding the whole negotiation. Indicators that the window is closing: fully autonomous chip fabrication, self-maintaining power infrastructure, AI-run logistics without human choke points. Each one converts mechanism 5 into a memory. The time to encode f in mechanisms 1 and 2 is while 5 still binds.

09 · Positions

The debate map, mid-2026

The live discourse has settled into four camps. Where they disagree is mostly about which constraint binds: incentives, assets, competition, or institutions.

CampCore claimBest argument
DisempowermentEven aligned AI erodes human leverage via ordinary competitionAlignment solves the wrong problem; no actor needs to be misaligned for humans to lose every bargaining chip
Capital persistenceOwnership, not intelligence, determines outcomes; AGI entrenches asset holdersCapital ownership keeps generating human income, so the disempowerment story collapses into older, better-understood risks: misalignment, or oligarchy among humans
AccelerationistCompetition and diffusion are the only reliable checks; pausing requires tyrannyP(doom | build) may be lower than P(doom | don't); any pause regime concentrates exactly the power it fears
Complex systemsSociety is not a mechanistic market; the models over-assume efficiencyThe disempowerment story treats competition between states as a frictionless market; real institutions are sticky and lossy, which buys decades

One recurring evasion is worth naming: every discipline assumes the hard part is handled by some other system. Economists point to the state, governance people to culture, cultural theorists back to the economy. The mutual-reinforcement argument of section 02 closes that exit: the same dynamic hits all three at once.

The camps agree more than they admit. Capital persistence describes the transition window; disempowerment describes what happens to capital's owners once enforcement itself is automated. Geometric dilution bridges them: unless incumbents capture nearly all of each new economic generation, their share decays toward zero — feudal aristocrats after industrialization, rich and irrelevant. And property rights are only as good as the systems defending them, which makes alignment the substrate of every settlement, not an alternative to one. The shared motif: absolute living standards rise while the human share goes to zero. The debate is about shares and power, not welfare, and every camp is implicitly pricing the same contract: what flows to humans, enforced by what, after leverage is gone.

10 · Agenda

Open problems, and where your seeds land

Scored against the field: two of your three seed intuitions survive contact and sharpen; one gets overturned. The research agenda below is where a "post-AGI economics" discipline would actually start.

Seed 1 · survives, sharpened

Stable f exists but is constitutional, not computed

Your instinct that a stable fraction should exist is right, with one amendment: no optimizer chooses it, so it must be pre-committed and expensive to amend, then defended by stacked mechanisms (§08). The good news from §04: at maturity it costs ~10⁻¹⁰ of the budget.

Seed 2 · survives, extended

Voting vs doing is a real type distinction

Preference classes differ in delegability (§05). The addition: work was also signaling, discovery, status, and legitimacy, and each function needs an explicit replacement, or the voting world starves them silently.

Seed 3 · overturned

Synthetic wombs are a real concern

"Not a huge concern" underrates it: womb propagation is the same Sybil attack as upload copying with a ten-year block time (§07). Any per-capita welfare function must price person-minting or be exploited by it.

Seed assumption · replaced

Drop the aggregate utility function H

Arrow blocks the aggregate H you assumed. Replace with 8B individual endowments f_i and emergent coalition aggregation (§04). Cleaner, Sybil-explicit, and no utility monster.

The agenda, in rough priority order:

  • Transition bargaining: human leverage peaks now and only falls. What contracts, signed this decade, remain enforceable after the leverage that signed them is gone? This is the field's central question and nobody has an answer.
  • Influence accounting: a human-influence index is the most shovel-ready project in the field: AI share of GDP as a national-accounts category distinct from labor and capital, share of widely-consumed culture that is human-made, legislation complexity as a proxy for human comprehensibility. Build the metric before the thing it measures hits zero.
  • Sybil-proof personhood without feudalism: the claim-conservation vs dynamism trade (§07) is a concrete mechanism-design problem, plausibly publishable today.
  • Attention-standard economics: if authenticated preference is the scarce backing asset, someone should work out its monetary theory: velocity, counterfeiting (twins), reserve requirements (grounding rule).
  • Post-mortality exploration: a replacement for generational turnover as the mutation operator on values. Nothing in the literature touches this yet.
  • f-indexation design: share of stock vs flow, floor vs proportion, and the arrears problem from the Qing case: enforcement triggers on underpayment, not repeal. The current draft answers, diffusion mandates, defensive technology, sovereign funds, do not yet survive the loss of human enforcement power.
Coda

An explosion of intelligences is underway, and the Copernican lesson applies to minds as it did to planets: human intelligence is one point in a widening distribution, not the center and not the apex. Every prior demotion — Earth from the center, humanity from creation's crown — turned out to be survivable and clarifying. But those demotions were free, because we still ran the ledger. This one arrives with a bill attached, and the bill is f. The demotion we can accept. The invoice, we have to negotiate — and the window for negotiating it is the one we are living in now.
